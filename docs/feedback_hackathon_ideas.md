When evaluating hackathon ideas, Kabir rejects ideas that:
- Are too similar to what the partner already does natively (e.g. GitLab Duo already does issue triage — don't build that for the GitLab track)
- Are pure analysis tools with no autonomous action ("why not just ask Claude?")
- Exist already as products (always search before committing to an idea)
- Are too niche in their wow moment (e.g. "cross-language detection" only matters during migrations)
- Feel like developer tools when the demo would bore non-technical judges

**Why:** Optimising purely to win, not for personal pain point or portfolio value.

What makes an idea sit right for him:
- Large scale (20+ agents like SalesShortcut's 34 agents — the grand prize winner of the ADK hackathon)
- Autonomous action, not just analysis (agents DO things in real systems)
- Clear "why not just ask Claude" answer (MCP access to private data + action at scale)
- Something genuinely novel that doesn't already exist
- A cinematic demo arc that works in 3 minutes

**How to apply:** Before suggesting a hackathon idea, verify it against: (1) does it already exist? (2) does it just warn/analyse or does it act? (3) is the scale genuinely justified by the problem?
